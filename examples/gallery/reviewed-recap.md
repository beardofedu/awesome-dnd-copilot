## Input: fictional notes, revision 1

> Write only a 150-250-word player recap in second person. Use only these
> established events. Do not add motives, rewards, causes, or player decisions.
> Leave unknowns unresolved. End with a question, not an assumed next action.
>
> Session 2, the invented town of Willowharbor. The party inspected a damaged
> ferry landing, found a loose mooring ring, and showed it to the ferrymaster.
> The ferrymaster closed the landing until repairs can be made. The party
> borrowed rope from the net-makers and used it to mark a safe waiting area.
> They returned the unused rope. A child pointed out painted arrows on the
> nearby towpath. The party followed the arrows to an empty boat shed.
> Inside was a map of the local waterways with one island left unnamed.
> The party left the map in place and arranged to meet the ferrymaster at
> the landing the following morning. Nobody established why the arrows were
> painted, who made the map, or what is on the unnamed island. These events
> are all intended to be known to the fictional players.

## Illustrative draft excerpt: not approved

> You uncovered a smuggler's map and agreed to sail to the unnamed island
> at dawn.

This deliberately flawed excerpt is a review exercise, not the agent's full
requested recap. It invents both a motive and a party decision.

## Simulated DM review

**Reject** the smuggler attribution and planned voyage: neither is in revision
1. **Retain** the map discovery and next-morning meeting. **Leave open** who
painted the arrows and what the island contains.

**Example sharing decision:** Accept the replacement below for the fictional
players only, based on the supplied events. This simulated decision does not
approve reuse in another campaign or public publication. The source notes,
bad draft, and review are not part of the player-facing extract.

## Final player-safe example: replacement recap

You began at Willowharbor's damaged ferry landing, where a loose mooring ring
gave you something concrete to show the ferrymaster. After seeing it, the
ferrymaster closed the landing until repairs could be made. You borrowed rope
from the net-makers, marked out a safe waiting area, and returned what you
had not used. The landing remained closed; no repair had yet been recorded.

Nearby, a child drew your attention to painted arrows along the towpath. You
followed those marks to an empty boat shed. Inside, you found a map of the
local waterways, including one island with no name written beside it. You
left the map where it was. Who made it, who painted the arrows, and what
might be on that island were still unanswered questions.

Before the session ended, you arranged to meet the ferrymaster back at the
landing the following morning. That meeting is your next agreed commitment,
not a decision to leave town or sail anywhere. With the landing closed and
the map still in the shed, what will you ask the ferrymaster when you meet?
