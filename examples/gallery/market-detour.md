# Encounter card: the wandering market

[Back to gallery](./README.md)

**Resource:** [Encounter Improv](../../running-campaign/agents/encounter-improv.md).
**Audience:** DM-only preparation. **Status:** Draft, not DM-approved or
player-facing. This is original fictional material, not a played encounter.

## Input

> Four level-3 adventurers in a hopeful D&D 5e game have ignored my planned
> route to visit the market in the invented town of Mossquay. Give me one
> brief, noncombat encounter card. A gust has tangled the market's directional
> ribbons. Keep the stakes local, allow several solutions, and do not force
> the party back onto my planned route. No roll, reward, or NPC relationship
> has been established.

## Illustrative output: draft

```text
SITUATION: The market's ribbon signposts now point in circles, and a delivery
          runner asks whether anyone can help locate the potter's stall.
STAKES: A delivery may arrive late; helping could earn a friendly introduction,
        but nobody's safety or the main adventure depends on this errand.
TWIST: Each trader knows the neighboring stall by its awning color, not its name.
RESOLUTION OPTIONS: Ask traders to trace the route together; climb an approved
                    lookout to spot the potter's kiln-shaped sign; or offer to
                    redraw the ribbon map. Declining remains a valid choice.
```

**Optional DM detail, proposed:** The runner carries empty display boxes,
not anything fragile or hazardous. Their immediate concern is arriving before
the potter packs up. Whether a lookout exists and can be used safely is for
the DM to establish; asking the traders needs no invented structure.

**Unresolved:** Time available, market layout, and any reward. Do not add a
mandatory check or claim this is balanced combat; no combat is proposed.
If a player tries something uncertain, adjudicate it using the table's agreed
rules edition rather than treating this card as a rules reference.

## Review before use

Confirm the market fits established geography and the table's tone. Keep
optional details provisional until accepted, and do not make helping the runner
a prerequisite for returning to the main adventure. Narrate only what the
characters can observe; this prep card is not a player handout.
